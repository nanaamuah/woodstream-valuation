    How the sales comparison approach works here

    The approach values a home by what buyers recently paid for similar homes nearby. Each sale is adjusted for the ways it differs from the subject, so that every adjusted price answers the same question: what would this sale have fetched if it were the subject?

    Adjustments are always made to the comparable, never to the subject. The rates above are my assumptions for a 1980s–1990s suburban market in Columbus at about $210–$250 per square foot. The living-area rate is set well below the average price per square foot, because the land and the basic structure are already paid for; extra square feet add less than the average.

    The grid shows what the five default sales imply for living area on their own. With so few sales and so many other differences, that raw slope is unstable, so I report it as a check and keep my rate.

    Reconciliation gives more weight to sales that needed fewer adjustments, because each adjustment adds uncertainty. The final figure is rounded to the nearest $1,000.
